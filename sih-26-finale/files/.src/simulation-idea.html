The Simulation Segment

Plain-words version · v2 of this document, with your answers from 14 Sep built in · SIH PS 26025

In one line

The simulator is a virtual mine. You give it a mine's data. It fills the gaps, places the sensor nodes and runs the mine. Then it lets you freeze the ground and create subsidence to see what happens, and it shows what an ML prediction means on the ground.

It generates, simulates and displays. It does not predict (ML does that), and it does not alarm, send SMS, sync offline or push app alerts (backend does those).

3. Job 1 · Virtual mine (v1, Tue 15 Sep)

  1. Mine data in: Adriyala mine. Panel 250 m × 2500 m, 375 m deep, 283 real measured points.
  2. Fill the gaps with a formula fitted to that real data (tilt, stretch, values between survey dates, sensor noise, radio loss). Everything filled in is tagged synthetic.
  3. Place nodes with the algorithm, based on the mine's shape and where the ground moves.
  4. Run 690 days. The coal face moves and the ground sinks.
  5. Nodes send data forward: a data file plus a live stream, some packets lost.
  6. Window 1: a 3D view of the ground sinking, with nodes, a day slider and a SIMULATED banner.

4. Job 2 · Freeze and create subsidence (v2, Wed 16 Sep)

How it works: in Window 1 you press Freeze at any day. Window 2 opens with the ground exactly as it is at that moment, and Window 1 keeps playing back the finished run, untouched. You click a spot and pick a type. The simulator reads the data near that spot at that moment and works out what happens: how much the ground there has already sunk, how much it can still sink, the depth, and the seam height.

#Scenario typeWhat decides the result (the "nearby data")When
1Sudden local sinkingThe sinking that hasn't happened yet at that spot happens all at once. Over old settled ground there's little left, so the result is small. Just behind the face there's a lot, so the result is big. Over solid coal there's no empty space, so it's "not possible here". Depth sets how wide it spreads.Wed · first batch
2Ground crackingHow fast the ground near the spot is moving now (last 24 h). Carry that on for N days, then show where the stretch is big enough to crack, and how wide.Wed · first batch
3Tilt (edge failure)The coal pillar at the nearest panel edge gives way, so the sunken area gets wider on that side. The ground next to the edge tilts sharply.Wed · first batch
4Blast vibrationDistance from the blast and charge weight give shaking speed (mm/s) at every point, checked against Indian DGMS limits. It needs two site constants from a published Indian study; until we find them, it says "not set" instead of guessing.Wed · second batch
5Sinkhole (old shallow workings)Void height, how much broken rock swells, and the cover depth. If the swollen rock fills the void first, the answer is "no sinkhole reaches the surface" and says why. (Sinkholes caused by water washing soil into old workings aren't modelled; the screen says so.)Wed · second batch

Every scenario shows the same results: extra sinking · tilt · stretch/squeeze (strain) · cracks with width · effect on each object on the ground · shaking, for blasts only. A plain-words summary sits on top.

Scenarios are labelled SCENARIO. They are stored in the simulation's own storage (a list of saved scenarios) and never sent to the backend or ML.

5. Job 3 · What a prediction means (v2, Wed 16 Sep)

We don't build the ML. We build the pipe: prediction in, meaning out.

 ML file (per node: sinking at +24 h and +72 h, low / likely / high)
     │  check the file is valid
     ▼
 spread the predicted change from the nodes over the ground ─► add it to today's ground
     ▼
 tilt · strain · cracks · objects  ─►  FORECAST VIEW, with a low / likely / high switch

Until the ML teammate's real file arrives, a test file built from the simulator is used. The screen says TEST INPUT, NOT A REAL FORECAST.

6. Objects on the ground (from research on Indian coalfields)

Coal areas in India (Jharia, Raniganj, Godavari valley) have villages, roads, railway lines, power lines and water pipes directly above the workings. The Dhanbad–Chandrapura railway line was shut in June 2017 because of the underground fire and subsidence risk in Jharia; it reopened in 2019 after local protests.

We only give a grade where a published limit exists. Everywhere else we show the physical number in plain words, e.g. "the top of the 10 m pole moves 12 cm". The objects are placed as an example village, not the real surface above Adriyala.

7. Rules that never change

  • Fake is never shown as real. Banners: SIMULATED SCENARIO FORECAST.
  • A scenario never changes the main run. Window 1 and the data file sent to the team stay exactly the same.
  • The simulator never raises an alarm. Damage grades describe the ground; they don't send alerts.
  • Same input gives the same result, every time.
  • No numbers typed into code. Every value lives in a settings file with its source.

8. Honest limits

  • One real mine dataset. The formula is about 27% low at the deepest point.
  • Scenario settings like pillar width, blast charge and old-working depth are your inputs, not measured data from this mine.
  • Some limits (roads, poles, rails, pipes) aren't sourced yet, so we show numbers only. Crack width is an estimate; the point where cracks start (about 3 mm per metre of stretch) is from published studies.
  • Houses near the panel edges already show damage grade II–III before any scenario. That's what longwall mining at this depth does, not a bug.
