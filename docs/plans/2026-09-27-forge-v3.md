## Context: what I found (all measured on 27 Sep)

1. **Why subsidence changes so fast.** `surface.S = _BASE_S · (1 − e^(−0.04·t))`
   (`simulation/sandbox/surface.py:274`), with the whole 250 × 2500 m panel treated as mined on
   day 0 (`_A = np.outer(_MASK_Y, _MASK_X)`, `surface.py:162`). The peak goes 0 → 0.66 m by day 10,
   1.10 m by day 20 and 1.98 m by day 120, starting at about 80 mm/day. At FORGE's 1 d/s that is
   8 cm of new bowl every second at the start. The module docstring (`surface.py:35`) planned a
   **travelling face**, but it was never built. → Build it (Adarsh chose this).
2. **The obvious way to add a face breaks the 3D view.** The frontend never receives S. It draws
   the bowl as `base_bowl_mesh × timeScalar` (`server.py:272-283` sends `_BASE_S` once, and `App.tsx:383` →
   `TerrainMesh`/`NodeMarkers`). With a moving face, the shape changes over time, not just its size.
   The fix works because the mask is separable: S = A_M · Px(x) · Py(y,t). The server sends Px
   (121 numbers) once and Py(t) (121 numbers) in every packet or frame. The frontend draws
   `Px[ix]·Py[iy]`, which stays cheap.
3. **The engine's tilt is 2.8× too big, and stays that way.** `collapse.py`:
   `yield_boost = 1 + 1.8·yield_frac` multiplies tilt and curvature, and `yield_frac` stays 1.0
   after the collapse. For a 0.5 m / 100 m bowl, the true peak slope is 3.03 mm/m, but the engine
   reports 8.49 mm/m. That is why "TILT 5 mm/m" gives 8.5 mm/m at the target. A strain-threshold
   crack rule built on these numbers would fire 2.8× too easily. → Fix the boost so it applies
   only before the collapse.
4. **The health strip goes back to green about 0.6 days after a cave-in.** `active_collapses`
   (`session.py:54-61`) keeps a failure only up to `t_collapse + duration + 0.05 d`, and
   `assign_node_states` colours nodes only for active failures. B3's "PLAY stops inside the
   window" only hid this. → In FORGE, keep the colours for any event that has started (Adarsh:
   damage doesn't heal). The live engine keeps its own rule.
5. **The stray radius circle on the SIM tab.** `App.tsx:148-158` starts with
   `targetLocation = (120, 80) "N-E Highland Ridge"`, `collapseRadiusM = 75` and
   `beaconHidden = false`. Both embed slots therefore draw a red 75 m ring that nobody asked for.
6. **Cave-in colours.** `TerrainMesh.tsx` scales depth linearly to `CUMULATIVE_DEPTH_MAX_M = 50`
   (`geomechanicsEngine.ts:79`), and the contour interval is 5 m (`TerrainMesh.tsx:21`). The 2 m bowl
   therefore uses 4% of the colour scale and gets no contours, and a 10 m cave-in uses 20%. Both
   look light blue.
7. **The four events are nearly the same thing today.** TILT is a cave-in shifted one radius
   (`sim-tab.js:679-709`), with the same dust, the same speed and the same round pit. CRACK does
   not exist, and the engine has no crack model (`CrackLinesOverlay` in `MineViewport.tsx:78` only
   draws). The "radius" is the Gaussian σ (`collapse._spatial_profile`), so the ground is still at
   61% of the depth at r = R and 13% at 2R.
8. **The mini map** is a hand-drawn 200 px SVG (`sim-tab.js:1242`), not the MAP tab. The MAP tab
   is Leaflet with local tiles at `http://127.0.0.1:8085/tiles/{satellite,labels,topo}/…`
   (`map-view.js:73-108`), projected with `mapView.xyToLatLon`. Its node icons come from
   `createIcon` in `node-markers.js:96`, which is not exported yet. `mapLayers.loadPanelOutline`
   keeps a single module-level layer, so the mini map must load the GeoJSON itself.
9. **The end day** is fixed at 120 in `forge/server.py` (`/forge/range`) and `sim-tab.js:765,781,924`.

## How the four events will differ (the answer to "what is the difference")

| | CAVE-IN | TILT | CRACK | VIBRATION |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Shape | round pit | one-sided slope, no pit at the target | a line you draw | none |
| Speed | hours (warning 8 h, collapse 5 h) | creeps over days (slider, 1–30 d) | opens over days (slider, 0.5–30 d) | 60 s |
| Maths | `PillarFailure` at the target | one wide `PillarFailure` placed so the tilt at the target **equals** the rate slider (fixed mapping) | a chain of narrow `PillarFailure`s along the line, scaled so the trench depth equals the THROW slider | `apply_vibration` PPV |
| 3D look | dust, shake, hot colour ramp, zone rings | direction arrow, no dust, amber slope tint | jagged crack lines, narrow trench | shockwave only |
| Node colour | zone rings + maths | maths (tilt/strain limits) | zone "capsule" along the line + maths | none |

**Node colour rule in FORGE (Adarsh's answer).** Around each started CAVE-IN or CRACK there is a
**white circle at 1.2R** and a **red circle at 1.5R**. Nodes inside the white circle are red
(CRITICAL). Nodes between the white and red circles are yellow (WARNING). Outside the red circle,
the maths decides, using only the change the event itself caused:
- WARNING if tensile strain ≥ `EPS_TENSILE_LIMIT` (5.3 mm/m), compressive strain ≥ `EPS_COMPRESS_LIMIT`
  (6.6 mm/m), or tilt ≥ 3 mm/m;
- CRITICAL if tilt ≥ 10 mm/m.

The tilt limits are new constants marked ASSUMED. The colour stays for as long as the event's
effect stays: forever for a pit or crack, once it has started. Knothe settlement on its own never
reddens a node, the same rule the engine uses.

**Automatic cracks.** Wherever the tensile strain in a frame passes `EPS_TENSILE_LIMIT`, the frame
also returns short crack segments running along the bowl rim, at right angles to the slope
direction. Cracks caused by an event are fresh red. Cracks from the settlement bowl alone are
orange. The lattice is fixed and the jitter seeded, so they don't flicker between frames.
